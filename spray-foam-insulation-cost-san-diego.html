How Much Does Spray Foam Insulation Cost in San Diego? (2026 Guide)

Straight talk on pricing — what you can expect to pay, what moves the number, and how to budget with confidence.

Updated April 2026 6 min read

Pricing is usually the first thing homeowners want to know — and the hardest to pin down online, because every home is different. Below are realistic 2026 ranges for San Diego, plus the honest factors that push a quote higher or lower. For an exact number, nothing beats a free on-site estimate.

How spray foam is priced: board feet

Spray foam is measured in board feet — one square foot of area covered at one inch of thickness. A quote depends on how much area you're covering and how thick the foam needs to be to hit your target R-value. As a general San Diego range in 2026:

  • Open-cell foam: roughly $1.50–$2.50 per board foot, installed.
  • Closed-cell foam: roughly $2.50–$4.50 per board foot, installed.

Closed-cell costs more because it uses more raw material and delivers a higher R-value per inch. These are ballpark figures — your project could land outside them in either direction.

Typical project ranges

To make it more concrete, here's what common San Diego jobs tend to run. Treat these as rough planning numbers, not quotes:

  • Attic (average home, roofline sealed): $3,500–$9,000+ depending on size and foam type.
  • Walls during a remodel (open cavities): $1.50–$3.00 per square foot of wall.
  • Crawl space / subfloor (closed-cell): $2,500–$7,000+ depending on square footage and access.
  • New construction: quoted per the plan set — ask us for a builder estimate.
The cheapest quote is rarely the best value. Under-applied foam that doesn't hit the right thickness won't perform — and fixing it later costs far more than doing it right once.

What drives your price up or down

1. Open-cell vs. closed-cell

The single biggest lever. Closed-cell is premium; open-cell stretches your budget further where its performance is enough.

2. Thickness and target R-value

More inches of foam means more material and higher cost. California's Title 24 energy code sets minimums, and we spec to meet or exceed them.

3. Square footage

Bigger areas cost more in total but often less per board foot, since setup and mobilization are spread across a larger job.

4. Access and prep

A tight, cluttered crawl space or a hard-to-reach attic takes more labor. Removing old, damaged insulation before we spray also adds to the total.

5. Removal of existing insulation

If we need to tear out and haul away failing batts or rodent-damaged material first, that's additional labor and disposal.

Don't forget the return

Spray foam is an investment, not just an expense. With cooling-cost reductions of up to 40% on many San Diego attic jobs, plus improved comfort and resale value, a well-installed foam job pays you back over time — and unlike batts, it won't need replacing.
